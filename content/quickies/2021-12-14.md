## Educating ... Patiently

This is a re-telling of a fascinating interaction I had while answering phones and providing tech support for the company I work(ed) for at the end of 2021. It was ... certainly something.

---

I had a call at my new job on Sunday that was weird as heck.

It started out normal. "Hello, thanks for calling, I'm Terra, what's the issue tonight?"

But a couple minutes in the man on the line asks "Terra, I notice that you seem really comfortable talking to men. A lot of women get kinda defensive about that."

I'm honest with him, but only enough to provide an answer and move forward. "Well, part of it is that I'm hella gay. Now let's have a look at fixing this part of the issue." And yes, I said the words 'hella gay'.

The call moves forward, and at some point he says something that I recognise as a transphobic dog whistle, and I politely say that biology is complicated re: gender and sex. He asks what I mean, and I explain: "There are people born with XX chromosomes who are assigned male at birth, and there are people born with XY chromosomes who are assigned female at birth. And that's just the start of it. These are well documented medical conditions." And I proceed to list off the names of some of them as I continue working to fix his problem.

The call moves on, and as I finish up, he stops me just before I'm about to end the call and says "Can I ask you something not at all business related?"

I'm nervous about what's coming, but I agree (mistake, but oh well).

"What's the deal with 'transvestite' and 'transgender'?"

Fuck. We're doing this. And I will almost never turn down a request for knowledge. So I start.

Transvestite is an outdated term that's now considered derogatory in a lot of the queer community and refers to the folks that we'd normally call cross-dressers, drag queens, and drag kings. Transgender refers to someone who identifies as a gender other than what they were assigned at birth.

He informs me that he's got no problem with that BUT he takes umbrage with the notion that trans women say they \*ARE\* women. He'll respect names and pronouns, but he just can't see trans folks as who they identify as.

I could take this in one of two ways. I could tell him straight up: trans women are women, trans men are men, deal with it. Or I could explain WHY trans folks say that. Knowing full well that there's no point in trying to convince him of the former, I go with the latter.

"Trans folks know all too well the reality of their body with regard to sex. One of the main reasons the language changed to absolute is that trans folks got a lot of push back about words like 'identify' when discussing gender. For all intents and purposes related to everyday interactions trans folks ARE who they say they are, and it has no impact on anyone else. This is one case where, whether or not you agree with the language, it makes sense to just adapt to it. It's causing no harm to anyone."

I was moderately shocked at how well he took it. We finally ended the call, and then I breathed a sigh of relief that it was over.
